From Probabilistic Marketing Claims to Cryptographic Market Invariants in Autonomous Enterprise Systems

Executive Summary: As enterprise AI transitions from human-in-the-loop copilots to autonomous multi-agent orchestration, classical software testing hits combinatorial walls. Brand trust has become an unsustainable PR exercise. Market leadership now belongs to firms that replace discursive marketing claims with quantum-accelerated formal verification paired with zero-knowledge attestation, turning baseline software safety into a verifiable, high-margin commercial asset.

I. The Epistemic Crisis of Autonomous Go-to-Market

Traditional enterprise software marketing relies on a social contract of assumed conformity: SOC 2 reports, penetration-testing summaries, benchmark leaderboards, and deterministic SLA guarantees. For next-generation multi-agent systems, where execution paths branch stochastically across LLM inference graphs, this contract fractures:

  • The Stochastic Drift Trap: Deterministic unit tests cannot cover semantic emergence. An agent workflow passing 99.9% of regression prompts can still execute a catastrophic latent logic traversal in an edge-case enterprise context.
  • The Signaling Lemons Problem: Because buyers cannot verify internal model alignment or safety bounds without exposing trade secrets, the market suffers from adverse selection. Safety claims degrade into generic marketing noise.
  • The GTM Penalty: Enterprises may stall procurement while conducting bespoke red-teaming and compliance audits. Speed-to-trust becomes a major bottleneck of enterprise ARR expansion.
